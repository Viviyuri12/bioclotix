Clinical Gap

Why Conventional Dressings Fall Short

Standard cotton gauze and single-agent hemostatic powders introduce critical limitations during acute wound care and dressing removal.

01

Painful Scab Tearing

Fibrous gauze weaves embed directly into forming clots. Removing the dressing pulls away fragile new tissue, causing severe pain and immediate secondary re-bleeding.

02

Slow Clot Stabilization

Single-mechanism agents rely on either passive fluid absorption or weak surface contact alone, delaying fibrin matrix formation across active, irregular wound beds.

03

Premature Moisture Clumping

Pre-mixed powders exposed to ambient humidity or fluid backflow clump inside single-chamber applicators, clogging tips when rapid deployment is needed most.

Synergistic Chemistry

Dual Plant-Derived Biomaterials

Housed separately until actuation, Chamber A and Chamber B combine complementary hemostatic pathways directly on the wound surface.

Chamber A Reservoir Plant Cellulose Polymer

Oxidized Regenerated Cellulose (ORC)

Derived from purified plant cellulose, ORC provides structural hemostatic reinforcement and natural tissue compatibility:

  • Acidic Microenvironment: Lowers local surface pH to promote platelet activation and help inhibit bacterial proliferation.
  • Physical Gelatinous Matrix: Swells upon contact with blood to form a cohesive, protective seal over irregular tissue contours.
  • Naturally Bioabsorbable: Breaks down cleanly via enzymatic hydrolysis and gentle saline rinse without disturbing healed skin.
Chamber B Reservoir Botanical Polysaccharide

Microporous Starch

Engineered botanical starch microspheres act as high-capacity molecular sieves that accelerate the body's intrinsic clotting cascade:

  • Rapid Plasma Absorption: Microscopic surface pores immediately draw water out of blood plasma within seconds of contact.
  • Clotting Factor Concentration: Dehydration concentrates endogenous platelets, red blood cells, and thrombin directly at the wound site.
  • Synergistic Gel Reinforcement: Integrates seamlessly with ORC fibers to lock in a dense, stable hemostatic plug.
Mechanical Delivery Architecture

Dual-Chamber Mechanical Applicator

Four integrated mechanical elements deliver simultaneous, moisture-protected powder dispersion without electronics or calibration.

01

Chamber A Reservoir

Hermetically isolated upper compartment storing dry Oxidized Regenerated Cellulose (ORC) powder free from ambient moisture.

02

Chamber B Reservoir

Dedicated lower compartment housing dry Microporous Starch microspheres separated by a rigid internal barrier.

03

Manual Pneumatic Pump

Ergonomic elastomeric bulb that generates balanced dual-channel airflow upon single-handed compression—zero power needed.

04

Fixed Precision Dispensing Tip

Streamlined converging nozzle that directs both powders onto the wound bed in a consistent, targeted plume.
